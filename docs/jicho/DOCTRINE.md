# Doctrine

## Pre-closure sequence

### FRAME
State the actual question without smuggling guilt into it.

### UNIVERSE
Define actors, jurisdictions, languages, time periods, asset classes, document classes and institutional roles that may matter.

### BLIND SPOT
Ask which categories the current framing makes invisible: aliases, proxies, relatives, intermediaries, former names, non-English sources, old records, physical outcomes, unexpected sectors.

### VIEWPOINT
Reconstruct the system from multiple positions: citizen, supplier, official, financier, beneficiary, reporter, auditor, victim, regulator, defendant.

### PROVENANCE
Every claim points to an original source, date, retrieval path and transformation history.

### RIVALS
Maintain leading, competing and null explanations.

### CAUSALITY
Do not upgrade association into sponsorship, control, corrupt purpose or criminal liability without evidence bridging the causal gap.

### INVERSION
Ask what the world should look like if the allegation is false.

### TRANSFER
Compare mechanisms across cases and jurisdictions, while recording where the analogy breaks.

### STOP
Close when new evidence has plateaued, rival models are explicit, remaining uncertainty is known, and the next search would add little information.

## Coverage confidence vs answer confidence

Every consequential output carries two independent judgments:

- **Answer confidence** — strength of evidence for the proposition.
- **Coverage confidence** — probability that relevant evidence classes were actually searched.

High answer confidence with low coverage confidence is not closure.

## Adversarial ontology

Assume sophisticated actors may exploit:
- near-identical entity names,
- aliases/transliterations,
- nominees and relatives,
- shell chains,
- jurisdiction switching,
- professional intermediaries,
- document-format silos,
- official-role vs actual-control gaps.

Entity resolution must be capable of both MERGE (different names, same entity) and SPLIT (similar names, different entities).

## Three clocks

Every public-interest case tracks:
1. **Legal clock** — investigation, charge, dismissal, acquittal, conviction, appeal, sanction.
2. **Evidentiary clock** — allegation, corroboration, documented discrepancy, finding, contradiction, exculpatory evidence.
3. **Narrative clock** — material public/political reframings, kept distinct from guilt or innocence.

Never collapse the clocks.
